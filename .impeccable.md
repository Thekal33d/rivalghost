## Design Context

### Users

RivalGhost is for arcade-game players discovering a short browser game through a hackathon demo, social post, or friend. They need to understand the immediate promise in seconds: move through danger, outscore a recorded rival, and take the Crown. Phone play is the primary context; desktop should feel like a deliberate arcade cabinet, not a stretched mobile screen.

### Brand Personality

Defiant, kinetic, and characterful. The game should create anticipation before a run, clarity during danger, and a satisfying sense of rivalry after a result. HOUND is an opponent with a visible personality, not a faceless leaderboard entry.

### Aesthetic Direction

Original neon-noir pixel arcade art: a restrained ink/navy base, hot gold for the Crown and score wins, ember red for hostile danger, and selective teal for safe interaction. Use crisp sprites, mechanical panel shapes, and purposeful motion. Avoid the flat monospace dashboard, anonymous dots, generic cyber gradients, and visual clutter from the rejected concept.

### Design Principles

1. Make the first five seconds playable, readable, and screenshot-worthy.
2. Put combat readability before decoration: player, projectiles, score gap, health, and Surge must survive a quick glance.
3. Let the Crown and HOUND create a rivalry story through motion, pose, and concise copy.
4. Make a portrait arena feel native on a phone and framed, not enlarged, on desktop.
5. Respect reduced motion and color contrast without weakening the game’s energy.
