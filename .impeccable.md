## Design Context

### Users

RivalGhost is for arcade-game players discovering a short browser game through a hackathon demo, social post, or friend. They need to understand the immediate promise in seconds: move through danger, outscore a recorded rival, and take the Crown. Phone play uses a fitted portrait arena. Desktop uses immersive edge-to-edge landscape flight, as requested in the November 12 design revision.

### Brand Personality

Defiant, kinetic, and characterful. The game should create anticipation before a run, clarity during danger, and a satisfying sense of rivalry after a result. HOUND is an opponent with a visible personality, not a faceless leaderboard entry.

### Aesthetic Direction

Illustrated arcade hangar: warm ivory, deep ink, vermilion armor, mint player accents, and crown gold. Original outlined vector pilots and matching canvas ships replace the rejected generated artwork and simple pixel mascot. Condensed headline lettering, mechanical silhouettes, slightly tilted character portraits, and restrained print-like borders create personality. Combat uses a dark teal arena for contrast. Avoid generic cyber gradients, fake activity, and decorative projectiles that do not match actual collisions.

### Design Principles

1. Make the first five seconds playable, readable, and screenshot-worthy.
2. Put combat readability before decoration: player, projectiles, score gap, health, and Surge must survive a quick glance.
3. Let the Crown and HOUND create a rivalry story through motion, pose, and concise copy.
4. Fit portrait flight to phones and rotate the same logical combat world into landscape flight on desktop; map controls to screen direction and keep sprites proportional.
5. Respect reduced motion and color contrast without weakening the game’s energy.
